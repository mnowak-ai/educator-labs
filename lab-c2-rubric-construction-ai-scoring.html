Step 2 of 11, sharpen the rubric

Two criteria are broken, in two different ways

Claim and Evidence have levels. A rater can point to the line that justifies a 3 instead of a 4. The other two do not, and that is where every problem in this lab comes from.

Reasoning: no levels

"Reasoning is well explained" tells a rater what to value but not how to tell a 2 from a 3. Every rater, human or AI, has to invent the levels. The AI invents different ones each time it runs. That is drift.

Communication: overlaps everything

"Clear and correct" covers grammar, but so does an AI's sense of what a "well explained" paragraph reads like. Grammar ends up scored twice: once here, and again inside Reasoning. That is bias, and it lands hardest on multilingual students.

Worth knowing before you blame the AI

When an AI scores something wrong, the first instinct is to say the AI made a mistake. Often it did. But just as often, the AI was handed a rubric that would confuse a human rater too, and it filled the gap the only way it could: by guessing. A vague criterion does not just make AI scoring unreliable; it makes every rater unreliable. So when you see a bad score in this lab, ask two questions in order: what did the rubric actually tell the rater to do, and only then, what did the AI do with it.

Step 6 of 11, calibrate and rerun

Give the two broken criteria real levels

Pick the descriptor set you would put in front of a new teacher and an AI on the same day. One option in each group is a trap.

Reasoning

Option A

4 = excellent reasoning; 3 = good reasoning; 2 = weak reasoning; 1 = no reasoning.

Option B

  • 4Explains why the data supports the claim, connecting a property of the towel (layers, thickness, material) to the measurements.
  • 3Explains why, but the connection to the data is general.
  • 2Restates the claim or the data without explaining why.
  • 1No reasoning.

Option C

  • 4Uses the word "because" and names the towel's structure in a complete sentence with no errors.
  • 3Uses "because" but has minor errors.
  • 2Does not use "because."
  • 1No reasoning.

Communication

Option A

4 = excellent writing; 3 = good writing; 2 = writing needs work; 1 = hard to read.

Option B

  • 4Grammar, spelling, and sentence structure never interfere with meaning. Score grammar here only, not under any other criterion.
  • 3A few errors, meaning still clear.
  • 2Errors sometimes make meaning hard to follow.
  • 1Errors make the paragraph hard to understand.

Option C

  • 4Zero grammar or spelling errors and at least five sentences.
  • 3One error.
  • 2Two or three errors.
  • 1Four or more errors.
