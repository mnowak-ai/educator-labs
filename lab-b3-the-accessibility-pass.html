Step 2 of 9 · The prompt · 4 minutes

What Kendra actually asked for

Thirty-second primer: prompts

A prompt is the instruction you type to an AI tool. The tool does what the words say, not what you meant. Vague words get you vague work. And if you ask it to confirm something, it will. That's not the same as it being true.

Here is the prompt Kendra used, one line at a time. Tap a line, then tap the bin it belongs in.

"Make this lesson accessible and UDL-compliant for students with disabilities."tap
"Keep it at a 7th-grade level."tap
"Confirm it meets WCAG 2.1 AA."tap
Does well
Missing something
Dangerous
Asking AI to confirm is asking it to grade its own work. It will say yes. It said yes to slide 4.
Missing: who, exactly. "Students with disabilities" isn't a person. Maya needs text for every image. Elena needs short chunks with headings. Jordan needs the lesson to change gears. None of that is in the prompt, so none of it happened on purpose.
Good instinct. Grade level is a real constraint. Notice it's the only specific thing in the whole prompt.
In your classroom
Whoever you ask to "confirm" something will confirm it. That's true of AI and of a tired colleague at 4:45.
